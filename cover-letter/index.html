A letter

The story
behind the CV.

A CV lists what I did. This is why I did it, what it taught me, and the kind of work I want to do next. It's the longer, more honest version.

Hello,

I have spent thirteen years taking products from an idea to something people use, across fintech, insurance, education, government and geospatial platforms in Egypt, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and the UAE. The most useful thing I can tell you is what changed recently. I stopped only directing engineers and started building again.

super-cv.me is a live AI product I designed, engineered and shipped on my own, in evenings and weekends around a full-time job. It runs on two model providers with a fallback between them, so it keeps working when one of them degrades. I am building a second now, TalentSphere, a video-first recruitment platform live in private seed. At my current company I shipped retrieval-based AI into our flagship platform, with an architecture where the model generates spatial queries without ever touching client data, and moved more than seventy percent of our design, documentation and development onto AI-assisted workflows.

The record before that is scale in difficult markets. I took a consumer wallet in Egypt from five thousand users to over a hundred thousand in a year, as the only senior product person in the company, under Central Bank of Egypt regulation, and grew engineering from seven to seventeen while doing it. In Saudi Arabia I led the research and the redesign of Tameeni, the Kingdom's largest motor insurance platform, used by more than ten million people a year. In Kuwait I led analysis and engineering on the national e-Driver's License system, which turned a paper process that took days into about ten minutes at a kiosk.

There is a pattern in that list I have stopped apologising for.

I do my best work in domains I do not know yet.

I had no GIS background when I built the platform I run now. I had never worked in digital identity before the licensing system, and I shipped a production AI product. What I offer is expertise on day one, maybe not in that exact domain, but I get useful quickly, and I build the thing that was not there before.

I hold a B.Sc. in Computer Science and I am completing a Marketing MBA, which is what lets me argue a business case as comfortably as a technical one.

An aside on taste

A few products I think are exceptionally designed: Linear, Apple Maps, GitHub, and Slack. Give me Apple Maps and I'd add speed-radar locations and lane guidance the way Waze does. Give me Linear and I'd let you open Claude from inside it to streamline the work. I can't help redesigning the things I use.

I am open to product leadership roles, remote or in the Gulf, and to relocation for the right team. My CV and portfolio are here, and I would be glad to talk.

Or, if you'd rather hear it from me:

Ahmed Elfateh.
Digital Product Lead · Cairo · Riyadh · MENA
