A letter

The story
behind the CV.

A CV lists what I did. This is why I did it, what it taught me, and the kind of work I want to do next. It's the longer, more honest version.

Hello,

I'm Ahmed, a product leader with more than 13 years in the digital space. The short story is on my CV. Here's the longer one.

My first real product lesson came from a national driver-licensing system. I was a developer turned analyst, and we took a process that ate days of paper and queues and turned it into about four minutes at a kiosk. The hard part wasn't the code. It was mapping every approval, every exception, every "but what if the applicant…" until the system carried the complexity so people didn't have to. I've been chasing that same feeling ever since.

At AevaPay I was the only product person on a wallet for unbanked segments. We grew it from 5,000 to over 100,000 users, largely by building a trust environment, KYC and the compliance features that make people comfortable moving their money.

At Rasan I led the redesign of Tameeni, Saudi Arabia's largest insurance platform. The version in the stores today, rated 4.7 stars by more than 157,000 people, is the one my team shipped.

One sharp user with one nailed job beats four half-served markets.

The thing I'm proudest of is SuperCV, because building it alone meant owning every decision, product, UX, pricing, and the AI architecture, and then living with all of them. What I'd do differently is focus. At AevaPay I ran four verticals with one small team, and I learned the expensive way that the sentence above is true. I'd make that trade earlier now, every time.

An aside on taste

A few products I think are exceptionally designed: Linear, Apple Maps, GitHub, and Slack. Give me Apple Maps and I'd add speed-radar locations and lane guidance the way Waze does. Give me Linear and I'd let you open Claude from inside it to streamline the work. I can't help redesigning the things I use.

Why this kind of work, and why now. I thrive in startups. I know product development and operations inside out, I'm an AI advocate, and I'm a genuine finance fan, especially after studying it academically in my MBA. I've traded on the side since 2020, which has put a lot of apps from a lot of countries in my hands, and taught me to notice the small decisions that make one feel trustworthy and another feel cheap.

If you're building something in product, fintech, or AI and any of this resonates, I'd love to talk. I think I can be effective at the messy problems, technical or commercial, and deliver real value.

Or, if you'd rather hear it from me:

Ahmed Alfateh.
Digital Product Lead · Cairo · Riyadh · MENA
