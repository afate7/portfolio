Phase 5 · Test & Ship

Category
Tameeni Rebrand · Phase 5
Year
2024
My role
Senior Product Manager → Research Manager
Team
5-person squad
🏁

The last phase is where research either proves it was worth the wall space or doesn't. Two disciplines closed the loop.

Step 1 — Test before believing

Deliverable: usability findings folded back into the designs.

The prototypes went in front of users before high fidelity hardened — walked through the eyes of the three personas from Phase 1: the first-time buyer, the Uber driver, the renewer. Sessions probed exactly the wounds the old product had: could someone compare two quotes now? Did the sequence-number explanation land at the moment of need? Did the restructured Basic Data tabs stop producing the confusion we'd watched in recordings? Findings went back into the wireframes — that loop, not any single screen, was the deliverable.

Step 2 — Ship, and keep testing

Deliverable: the native app, plus an A/B discipline for what remained arguable.

Some calls no lab session settles — ordering, defaults, copy. Those were marked for A/B testing in the live product (it's the entire content of the "Test" section on the process board, and it was meant that way: testing didn't end at launch, it became how the product argues with itself). The app that shipped replaced the webview outright: native, bilingual, design-system-built, with the quotation journey rebuilt against its drop-off walls — and no footer on the home screen.

Where it stands — dated, because numbers without dates are folklore

The project's working numbers (the drop-offs, the session times, the funnel losses) belong to the Nov 2023 – Feb 2024 window; they were the "before," and they're presented that way throughout this case. The "after" that can be verified publicly, checked on the Google Play listing in July 2026:

  • 4.7★ from 157,000+ ratings
  • 1M+ downloads
  • Two years after ship, in a category nobody enjoys — the rating held

I left Rasan in mid-2024, so the app's continued evolution belongs to the team that carried it. The rating holding is the part I'll claim a share of: it means the foundation — the flows, the system, the trust — was load-bearing.

What this project taught me

A revamp is earned, not demanded. The webview app had been an open secret for years. What changed wasn't the app; it was the argument — instrumented funnels, recorded sessions, a competitor teardown. "We should rebuild this" became a business conclusion instead of a designer's wish.

Weight problems, or drown in them. The step everyone had opinions about (the homepage) was losing 13%. The step almost nobody talked about (the quote list) was losing 21.7%. Without weighting, we'd have polished the wrong room.

Constraints wear disguises. Half the "requirements" we inherited were regulation; the other half were habit dressed as regulation. Interrogating which was which freed more screen space than any layout trick.

The footer is never just a footer. Every product has one — the detail that quietly confesses what the product really is underneath. Finding yours before your competitor points it out to your users: that's the job.
