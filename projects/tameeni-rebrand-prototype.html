Phase 4 · Prototype

Category
Tameeni Rebrand · Phase 4
Year
2024
My role
Research Manager
Team
5-person squad
📐

Everything before this phase was argument; this phase was construction. The process board pinned the sequence in advance — user flow → information architecture → lo-fi → hi-fi → prototype → usability testing → mockup — with a sticky note carrying the phase's one law: "consult with engineers to know the feasibility of the designs."

The build sequence, drawn before the build: user flow → IA → lo-fi → hi-fi → prototype → usability testing → mockup.

Step 1 — Information architecture, twice

Deliverable: two complete IA variants and a decision.

The forced-login versus optional-login question from Phase 3 was too consequential to settle by taste, so we drew both architectures in full: what the app becomes when it knows who you are from the first screen, versus what it becomes when it earns that knowledge later. Mapping both exposed the real costs — the forced variant simplified renewals and saved data but taxed the first-time buyer persona at their most fragile moment. The structure decided what opinion couldn't.

Step 2 — Wireframe the whole product

Deliverable: prototyped wireframes for every screen of the app.

Not the "key screens" — all of them. Onboarding, Arabic/English selection, OTP login, the full quotation journey step by step, the quote list with comparison, add-ons, vehicle photos, policies, renewal, the new "More" space that replaced the invisible sidebar. Each wireframe answered a specific problem wall from Phase 2, and each was wired into a clickable prototype rather than left as a picture.

The quotation journey rebuilt: one decision per screen, progress always visible — the anti-31-minute-session design. The quote list, the 21.7% step, redesigned around the moment of choice: comparable cards, the price filter where the thumb expects it, details one tap deeper.

Step 3 — The identity

Deliverable: a moodboard-driven visual direction and a design pillar.

The rebrand proper started here — not as a coat of paint but as a direction chosen against the research. The pillar we committed to in writing was "Clarity and Simplicity," and the moodboard translated it: calm surfaces, honest hierarchy, one primary action at a time, an identity finally designed for a native product instead of inherited from a website. The user goals it served were written on the same board: receive options flawlessly, compare independently with confidence, buy quickly and safely without cognitive dissonance, feel supported after the purchase.

The moodboard behind the new identity — card styles, plan presentation, coverage-benefit listing, policy management.

Step 4 — The design system and the wall

Deliverable: components, tokens, both languages, both directions — then every screen.

High fidelity meant a real design system first: components and tokens built bilingual and bidirectional from birth, because 57% of the audience lives in RTL. Screen design flowed from the system, not the other way around. By the end, the design file was a city — the section holding the flows and screens measures over 50,000 pixels wide.

The design wall at maximum zoom-out. Every path a user can take, drawn before it was built.
