Nagwa, Building EdTech That Actually Teaches

The Mission

Nagwa's proposition is simple and ambitious: high-quality educational content, free, for every student in the MENA region. The products that would deliver on that mission, Nagwa Classes (a live tutoring platform) and Nagwa Readers (a structured reading tool), were concepts waiting to become real.

My role was to take them from concept to shipped product, while also addressing the underlying content pipeline that made Nagwa's model possible.

Nagwa Classes

Live tutoring platforms have a two-sided market problem: you need students and tutors simultaneously, and the experience has to work for both. Early sessions showed students dropping off when technical issues disrupted the flow, and tutors struggling with tools not designed for the specific demands of one-on-one teaching.

We ran user research sessions with students and tutors separately, then together. The patterns that emerged were consistent: trust was built in the first 90 seconds of a session, and lost immediately when anything felt unreliable. We rebuilt the session experience around stability, technically and experientially, and redesigned the tutor interface to support the rhythms of actual teaching rather than generic video calls.

Nagwa Readers

Reading comprehension products are easy to build badly. The instinct is to add gamification, streaks, and rewards, to make reading feel like a game. What we found in research was more nuanced: students didn't want reading to feel like work, but they also didn't want it to feel trivial. They wanted to feel capable.

We designed Nagwa Readers around progressive challenge, each session calibrated to the student's current level, with enough stretch to create a genuine sense of achievement without triggering the frustration that leads to abandonment. The interface was quiet, deliberate, and respectful of the act of reading.

Content Pipeline Transformation

Nagwa's competitive advantage was the volume and quality of its content. But producing that content at scale required a workflow that had become a bottleneck. Editors, designers, and subject matter experts were doing repetitive work that didn't require human judgment.

We introduced AI-assisted automation at the right points in the content pipeline, automated formatting, initial tagging, template generation, freeing the human team to focus on the work that actually required expertise. The result: a 20% increase in content throughput without adding headcount.

Results

  • Nagwa Classes and Nagwa Readers both launched, opening a new B2C revenue stream to offset B2B volatility
  • Nagwa Classes has since passed 1M+ downloads
  • 20% increase in content production throughput through AI-assisted workflows
  • Content team able to focus on quality and curation rather than mechanical production
