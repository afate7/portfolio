The Power of Habit

Charles Duhigg Habits & Behavior

Why this book

Products live or die on the second visit, not the first. Duhigg's habit loop — cue, routine, reward — is the cleanest model I've found for why some products become part of someone's day and others get opened once and forgotten.

What stuck with me

Habits don't disappear; they get replaced. You can't delete a user's old behavior (paper forms, cash, the agent they always call). You can only offer a new routine that answers the same cue with a better reward. The driver-licensing project worked because it replaced a ritual, not because it removed one.

Keystone habits cascade. One small behavior change unlocks others. In teams, the keystone habit I install first is always the same: no feature discussion without naming the user problem. Everything else follows from it.

The craving is the engine. A reward the user doesn't anticipate isn't a reward. Progress indicators, confirmation moments, streaks — they work when they create anticipation, not decoration.

Where I apply it

Retention design, team rituals, and my own routines — the trading journal and the reading habit that produced this page both run on deliberately-built loops.
