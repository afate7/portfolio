Predictably Irrational

Dan Ariely Behavioral Economics
🎭

Why this book

This is the book I hand people when they ask why I care so much about behavioral economics. Ariely's core claim — that we're irrational in systematic, repeatable ways — is the single most useful idea I've carried into product work.

What stuck with me

Relativity governs choice. People don't know what things are worth; they know what things are worth next to other things. Every pricing page, every plan comparison, every "recommended" badge I've designed since reads differently once you accept this.

Free is a price with its own physics. Zero doesn't behave like a very low number — it short-circuits the cost-benefit math entirely. In the wallet product, waiving a tiny fee moved behavior far more than the fee's value could ever explain.

The pain of paying is real design material. Users don't experience a 20 EGP fee as 20 EGP; they experience the moment of paying. Restructuring when and how that moment happens changes completion rates without changing the price.

Where I apply it

KYC onboarding, checkout flows, plan pages, and every time someone in a roadmap meeting says "users will obviously choose the cheaper option." They won't. They'll choose the one that feels safest relative to its neighbors.
