The Upside of Irrationality

Dan Ariely Behavioral Economics

Why this book

If Predictably Irrational is about how users misjudge value, this one is about how builders do. Ariely turns the same lens on work, motivation, and attachment — and it reads like a diagnosis of every product team I've ever sat in.

What stuck with me

The IKEA effect is why teams ship the wrong thing. We overvalue what we made because we made it. The $500K roadmap audits, the features nobody used but nobody would kill — that's not stupidity, it's ownership bias, and knowing its name makes it negotiable.

Meaning beats money as a motivator — and its absence kills. Ariely's experiments on "Sisyphic" work (build it, watch it get shredded) explain team morale better than any HR framework. People don't burn out from hard work; they burn out from undone work.

Adaptation cuts both ways. Users stop noticing improvements shockingly fast. The delight you shipped last quarter is this quarter's baseline. Plan for it.

Where I apply it

Roadmap reviews (naming the IKEA effect out loud defuses it), team rituals that make finished work visible, and pacing improvements so they're felt rather than absorbed.
