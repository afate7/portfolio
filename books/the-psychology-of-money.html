The Psychology of Money

Morgan Housel Money & Behavior

Why this book

I've spent years building products that touch people's money — wallets, insurance, payments — and trading my own since 2020. This is the book that connects both sides: how people actually behave around money, versus how spreadsheets assume they behave.

What stuck with me

"Nobody is crazy." Everyone's money decisions make sense given what they've lived through. A user who refuses to keep a balance in a digital wallet isn't ignorant — they may have watched a bank freeze their family's savings. You can't design trust without respecting that history.

Wealth is what you don't see. The flashy metric is rarely the healthy one. In products, the equivalent is vanity metrics: downloads look like success the way a leased sports car looks like wealth.

Room for error is a strategy, not a weakness. Housel's case for margin of safety changed how I trade and how I roadmap. Plans that require everything to go right are plans that fail.

Where I apply it

Every fintech onboarding conversation, and my own trading journal. Also the reason I stopped arguing with users' "irrational" caution and started designing for it.
