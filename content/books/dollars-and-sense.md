## Why this book

The most practical of Ariely's books for anyone building payment or pricing experiences. It catalogs the specific mental shortcuts people use around money — anchoring, mental accounting, pain of paying, relativity — with enough texture to design against.

## What stuck with me

**Mental accounting is the user's real ledger.** Money isn't fungible in people's heads: "salary money," "bonus money," and "wallet balance" get spent by different rules. A digital wallet isn't competing with a bank — it's competing for a *mental account*, and it has to earn a label like "my daily money" to survive.

**Anchors are set whether you set them or not.** The first number a user sees becomes the reference for everything after. If your product doesn't choose the anchor deliberately, the market — or the user's last bad experience — chooses it for you.

**Payment pain is a dial, not a constant.** Prepaid feels different from postpaid; bundled feels different from itemized. Same money, different experience. That dial is a legitimate design surface — and an ethical one, per the dishonesty book.

## Where I apply it

Pricing pages, fee displays, top-up flows, and my own budget — which, yes, runs on deliberately-labeled mental accounts now.
