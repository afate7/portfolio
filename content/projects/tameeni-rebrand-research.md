There's a moment in every revamp argument where someone says *"I think users struggle with…"* and someone else says *"I don't think they do."* Meetings die there. So before we argued about anything, we made the product testify against itself.

## The instrumentation

We set up Google Analytics events across the entire funnel and paired them with Hotjar session recordings — not a sample week, but a sustained watch. The two tools answer different questions: GA tells you **where** people leave, Hotjar shows you **what their cursor was doing when they gave up**.

![Google Analytics analysis board, zoomed out](/assets/cases/tameeni/stage-analytics.png)
*The Google Analytics board — questions, hypotheses, and event data by the wall-full. Zoomed out deliberately; the shape of the work matters more than the numbers here.*

The funnel bled at every stage — double-digit drop-off on step after step, **15% on the homepage alone**. Not one catastrophic screen, but a toll booth at every gate. Users would arrive intending to buy — motor insurance is mandatory in Saudi Arabia, this is the closest thing to guaranteed demand a product can have — and the product still lost them, percent by percent, all the way to payment.

![Hotjar analysis wall, zoomed out](/assets/cases/tameeni/stage-hotjar.png)
*The Hotjar wall: recordings and heatmap findings for every funnel step, organized screen by screen.*

The recordings were the humbling part. Watching a real person rage-tap a webview screen that hasn't responded, scroll past a website footer inside the "app," or abandon a policy-number field they couldn't get right — that footage did more to align stakeholders than any deck I've ever written.

## Knowing the enemy

The trigger for all of this was a recent competitor eating our lead, so we studied them the way they'd studied us. The competitor teardown became one of the largest sections on the board: journey-by-journey comparison, feature audit, pricing presentation, onboarding friction — theirs against ours, step by step.

![Competitor analysis board, zoomed out](/assets/cases/tameeni/stage-competitors.png)
*The competitor teardown. It's long because being honest about a competitor's advantages takes more wall than dismissing them.*

We widened the lens too: market size and segmentation, Porter's five forces, and a market survey — because "our app is worse than theirs" and "our position is defensible" are different problems with different budgets.

## What research earned

By the end, nobody in the room was saying *"I think."* We had the drop-off number for every step, the recordings behind each number, and a competitor map showing exactly where we were losing. The revamp stopped being a request and became a conclusion.

That evidence base is what the next chapter spends: the autopsy of the old app itself.
