The app in the stores today is the one this process produced. Native, bilingual, built from the design system up — no webview, no page loads posing as screens, and no footer on the home screen.

## The numbers

- **4.7★** rating from **157,000+ users** — for a product category people use under deadline and rarely with joy
- **1M+ downloads** on Google Play
- A quotation journey rebuilt end to end, with the per-step drop-off walls it was designed against
- A design system and bilingual identity the product finally owns, instead of borrowing from the website

The rating is the number I keep. Insurance apps don't get loved by default — nobody wakes up excited to renew third-party liability. A 4.7 in this category means the product stopped being the obstacle between a person and a legal obligation. That was the entire brief, distilled.

## What I'd tell you over coffee

**A revamp is earned, not demanded.** The webview app had been an open secret for years; what changed wasn't the app, it was the argument. GA numbers, Hotjar footage, and a competitor teardown turned "we should rebuild this" from a designer's wish into a business conclusion that survived every meeting it entered.

**Losing ground is a gift, briefly.** The competitor did us a favor: they created the urgency that research alone couldn't. The window where a market leader is scared enough to act but still strong enough to win — that's when revamps happen. Wait longer and you're not revamping, you're catching up.

**Attach a metric or it's decoration.** The HMW walls with drop-off rates at the top were the most useful political tool of my career. They didn't just filter our ideas — they filtered everyone's, symmetrically, which is why they were trusted.

**The footer is never just a footer.** Every product has one: the detail that quietly confesses what the product really is underneath. Finding yours before your competitor points it out to your users — that's the job.
