Ideation with a blank whiteboard produces features. Ideation against a competitor teardown and a weighted problem backlog produces **positions** — deliberate answers to "why will a user choose us at each step." This phase built those positions.

## Step 1 — Tear down the local competition

**Deliverable: a journey-by-journey competitive audit.**

The trigger for the whole revamp was a competitor taking ground, so we made the study systematic: **Shory, Bcare, Thiqa, and Eltizam**, walked end to end, screen by screen, against Tameeni as the baseline. Two dimensions ran in parallel — the business layer (pricing, product offering, target audience, cross-selling, claims) and the experience layer (mobile experience, flow, navigation, brand identity, tone, clarity).

![Competitor analysis board, zoomed out](/assets/cases/tameeni/stage-competitors.png)
*The teardown wall. It's long because taking a competitor seriously requires more wall than dismissing them.*

The blunt summary: the newer players had **native-quality mobile experiences and cleaner single-purpose flows**, while Tameeni had the brand, the insurer network, and the traffic. The race was whether we could fix experience faster than they could build trust.

## Step 2 — Benchmark beyond the border

**Deliverable: an international pattern library with verdicts.**

We refused to let "best in Saudi" be the ceiling, so we benchmarked five international aggregators and recorded a verdict for each: **Comparethemarket** (long forms made tolerable by tabular selection; a rewards program doing retention work), **GoCompare** (free excess cover as a differentiator on every quote), **Yallacompare** (quote results opening with the cheapest of each category — TPL and comprehensive side by side), **The Zebra / Compare.com** (one question per screen — calm but patience-hungry; a cautionary tale, not a template), and **Sigortam.net** (an occupation-based discount as a hook, wrapped in a dated UI — proof that one sharp mechanic can outweigh polish).

## Step 3 — The opportunity workshop

**Deliverable: solution principles with owners in the room.**

Findings then went into a workshop with stakeholders and the engineering team — presented, argued, and converted into directions. A sticky from the process board describes the intent exactly: *"Present all the findings, to come up with ideas along with stakeholders and dev team."* The principles that survived the room:

- **Simplify the moment of choice.** Strip the quote card down to what picking an offer requires; let details live one tap deeper. Make comparison physically possible again.
- **Design for the two buyers we actually have.** The insight that reorganized the quote list: **TPL buyers shop on price; comprehensive buyers shop on quality.** One list, two reading modes — cheapest-first clarity for one, insurer ratings and policy highlights for the other.
- **Move the furniture users never found.** The invisible sidebar became a proper "More" destination; homepage content that nobody scrolled to moved into the entry pages where its audience actually was.
- **Explain before you ask.** Sequence numbers, editable IDs, photo requirements — every place research caught confusion earned an explanation at the moment of need, not a tooltip nobody clicks.
- **Respect the broker.** The Back-button-heavy comparison behavior belonged to a real professional segment; sharing and re-quoting became designed paths instead of accidents.

Two information-architecture philosophies also entered the ring here — a **forced-login** experience versus an **optional-login** one — a genuinely hard trade-off (personalization and saved data versus friction at the gate) that Phase 4 would resolve with structure rather than opinion.
