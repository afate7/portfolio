Research had named the disease; this chapter is the surgery. We ran the redesign through a discipline we'd committed to on the process board: **Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test**, with a rule attached — no design conversation without a metric in the room.

![The prototype stage of the process map](/assets/cases/tameeni/method-prototype.png)
*The method, drawn before the work: user flow → information architecture → lo-fi → hi-fi → prototype → usability testing → mockup. The sticky note is the project's second rule: "consult with engineers to know the feasibility of the designs."*

## Problems with numbers attached

Each step of the purchase journey — homepage, basic data, quote list, payment — got its own How-Might-We wall: the problem as observed, our thinking, the goal, and **the metric that would prove us right or wrong**. The Hotjar drop-off number for that step sat at the top of each wall like a bounty.

![Per-step HMW walls with drop-off rates, flowcharts and open questions](/assets/cases/tameeni/stage-problem-statement.png)
*One wall per journey step: drop-off rate, problem → thinking → goal → metrics, the flowchart of the step, and the open questions we owed stakeholders answers to.*

This structure did the political work as much as the design work. When a stakeholder wanted a feature, the wall asked the same question it asked us: which metric does it move? Ideas that couldn't answer didn't survive — including some of ours.

## Redrawing the whole product

Then came the rebuild: information architecture first, then wireframes for **every screen of the app** — onboarding, Arabic/English language selection, OTP login, the full quotation journey mapped step by step, quote list with comparison, add-ons, vehicle photos, policies, renewal. Not a facelift of the webview's pages; a native product designed from the flows outward.

![Wireframe: quotation journey step one](/assets/cases/tameeni/wire-quote-step1.png)
*The quotation journey, rebuilt: one decision per screen, progress always visible.*

![Wireframe: the quote list with comparison](/assets/cases/tameeni/wire-quote-list.png)
*The quote list — the moment of truth in an aggregator — redesigned for comparison instead of scrolling endurance.*

The wireframes were prototyped and walked, clickable, into two rooms: usability sessions with users, and feasibility sessions with engineers. A sticky note from the process board captures the second rule of the project: *"consult with engineers to know the feasibility of the designs."* Nothing shipped to high fidelity that the platform team hadn't already said yes to.

## The design wall

High fidelity meant a real design system — components, tokens, both languages, both directions — and screen designs flowing from it. By the end, the design file was a city.

![The design wall: user flows and screen designs, zoomed way out](/assets/cases/tameeni/stage-design-wall.png)
*User-flow diagrams feeding the screen designs. At full zoom this section is over 50,000 pixels wide.*

What all of it added up to — the shipped app and its numbers — is the final chapter.
