## Overview

Atlas started as a question: why does navigating a city still feel like reading a treasure map? GPS technology has been in our pockets for 15 years, yet the experience of finding your way through an unfamiliar neighborhood remains cognitively exhausting.

The client came to us with an existing app — functional, technically sound, user-retention hovering around 18%. They wanted a redesign. What we delivered was closer to a rethink.

## Discovery

Six weeks of research before a single wireframe. We shadowed 23 urban commuters across three cities, watching how they actually used navigation apps in real life — not in lab conditions, but on crowded sidewalks, in noisy subway stations, in the rain.

What we found: the apps were designed for cars. Turn-by-turn worked great at 30mph. On foot, at walking pace, in an environment that changes every few seconds, it created constant cognitive interruptions.

## The Design Insight

The key insight came from watching a participant navigate entirely by feel — glancing at the phone, pocketing it, walking, checking again. She wasn't reading directions. She was using the app as a compass.

We redesigned around that mental model. Instead of step-by-step instructions, Atlas shows **directional awareness** — where you are, which way you're facing, and a gentle pull toward your destination. Instructions only surface when you need to make a decision.

## Results

- **40% reduction** in cognitive load (measured via task completion stress tests)
- **3× user retention** at 30-day mark vs. previous version
- **4.8 stars** on App Store within 3 months of launch
- Webby Award for Best User Experience, 2024
